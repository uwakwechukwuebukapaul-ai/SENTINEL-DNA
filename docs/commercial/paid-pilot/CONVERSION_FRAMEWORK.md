# Commercial Conversion Framework

## Stage model

```text
Free Design Partner → Paid Pilot → Annual Subscription → Enterprise Expansion
```

Each transition is a decision gate. Participation, usage, positive feedback, or a sales conversation does not create an obligation to buy.

## Stage definitions and conversion triggers

| Stage | Exit evidence | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Free Design Partner | Validation record, feedback, scope, and readiness assessment complete | Program owner |
| Paid Pilot | Signed order form, paid terms, security/onboarding gates, and bounded success criteria complete | Commercial owner + security owner |
| Annual Subscription | Customer-approved closeout, documented business case, procurement path, and new order form | Account owner |
| Enterprise Expansion | Approved need for additional teams, controls, integrations, or Trust Fabric capabilities with security review | Account owner + product/security |

### Paid Pilot triggers

- A design partner has a documented workflow worth testing further.
- The customer sponsor accepts the pilot question, scope, fee, and time commitment.
- Security, legal, procurement, tenant, and evidence prerequisites have an owner and path to completion.
- The customer agrees to review evidence and record a decision.

### Annual subscription triggers

- The customer identifies a recurring workflow and accountable operating owner.
- Closeout evidence supports a customer-defined value hypothesis, with limitations recorded.
- Required procurement, security, privacy, and legal actions are understood.
- The customer accepts recurring scope, service, support, data, and renewal terms.

### Enterprise expansion triggers

- A named customer requirement needs broader users, environments, integrations, governance, or evidence operations.
- The expansion has an approved business case and does not rely on an unsupported outcome claim.
- Security architecture, tenant isolation, data handling, and operational ownership are re-reviewed.

## Pricing conversations

Lead with the customer’s defined workflow, scope, evidence obligations, support requirement, and buying process. Use the current approved price card and separate recurring subscription from one-time services. Do not use discounts, credits, pilot-to-subscription promises, or expansion pricing without approval.

## Procurement process

Record the budget owner, security review, privacy review, vendor onboarding, legal redlines, purchase order or equivalent, target date, and blockers. Procurement readiness is a tracked state, not a prediction of close.

## Renewal strategy

Begin renewal review before the pilot closes. Compare contracted scope with actual use, evidence quality, support needs, unresolved blockers, and customer decision. Renewal requires customer approval and an authorized order form; it is not automatic.

## Expansion opportunities

The core Sentinel DNA subscription remains the primary revenue model. Potential premium expansion capabilities are described in `TRUST_FABRIC_COMMERCIAL_LAYER.md` and require separate customer need, pricing, security, and legal review.

## CRM handoff fields

- Stage and stage-entry date.
- Customer sponsor, security owner, budget owner, and procurement owner.
- Validated workflow and evidence references.
- Package, scope, fee status, term, and approval references.
- Known blockers and next action.
- Customer decision: continue, stop, extend, defer, or expand.
- Claim/proof approval status.

## Loss and pause reasons

Use factual categories such as no budget, timing, security blocker, data constraint, workflow mismatch, insufficient evidence, procurement delay, competing priority, or customer decision not to proceed. Never relabel a non-conversion as a product success.
