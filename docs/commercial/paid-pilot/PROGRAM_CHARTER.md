# Paid Pilot Program Charter

## 1. Charter status and purpose

This charter defines the Sentinel DNA Design Partner → Paid Pilot program. It is an internal operating framework and a starting point for customer-specific proposal and legal review. It does not promise performance, compliance, cost savings, or conversion.

The pilot exists to test a narrowly defined cybersecurity workflow with a paying customer under explicit controls. The primary outputs are customer-reviewed evidence, an auditable operating record, analyst feedback, and a commercial decision—not a certification or an autonomous security decision.

## 2. Pilot objectives

- Validate the agreed investigation workflow against a documented baseline.
- Assess evidence completeness, provenance, replayability, and decision traceability.
- Measure analyst trust, usage, workflow fit, and perceived usefulness using the scorecards in this package.
- Verify tenant, access, audit, data-handling, and revocation controls in the customer-approved environment.
- Identify blocked workflows and required product or process changes.
- Give the customer sufficient evidence to decide whether to stop, extend, or evaluate an annual subscription.

## 3. Eligibility criteria

A candidate must have completed, or have a documented exception to, the Design Partner validation stage and must satisfy all of the following:

- An identified business sponsor, security owner, analyst lead, and procurement/legal contact.
- A bounded use case with named users, environment, data classes, integrations, and decision points.
- Capacity to attend kickoff, recurring reviews, and closeout.
- Approved synthetic or sanitized data for the initial scope. Any production-data use requires written approval and a separate data review.
- A security and privacy review path appropriate to the customer.
- Agreement that Sentinel DNA is advisory and does not replace analyst judgment or customer controls.
- A willingness to document measured results, including negative or inconclusive results.

The program owner may reject or pause a candidate when a critical security, privacy, legal, procurement, or operational dependency is unresolved.

## 4. Customer prerequisites

Before an order form is accepted, the customer must provide or approve:

- Named pilot sponsor and accountable operator.
- Approved scope, duration, package, fees, success criteria, and stop conditions.
- Tenant and RBAC ownership, least-privilege access, and access-review cadence.
- Data classification and retention decision.
- Evidence custody location and authorized reviewers.
- Audit logging, incident escalation, and support contacts.
- Origin and integration allowlists where relevant.
- A plan to revoke all pilot access and export or delete customer artifacts at closeout.

## 5. Scope boundaries

The paid pilot does not:

- Authorize autonomous containment, remediation, account creation, login automation, or changes to customer systems.
- Establish a security certification, regulatory attestation, or guarantee of detection, prevention, response time, or risk reduction.
- Permit unapproved data, credentials, secrets, browser sessions, or third-party content.
- Permit public references, case studies, or performance claims without the Customer Proof System approvals.
- Convert a design partner into a customer automatically.

All out-of-scope requests require documented change control and security review.

## 6. Duration options

The order form selects one approved duration and defines entry and exit gates. The following are planning options, not commitments:

| Option | Use | Required review points |
|---|---|---|
| Short | One bounded workflow and a small analyst group | Kickoff, midpoint, closeout |
| Standard | Multiple related workflows with recurring evidence review | Kickoff, weekly operating review, midpoint, closeout |
| Extended | Broader workflow and integration validation | Kickoff, weekly review, formal control reviews, executive closeout |

Exact calendar dates, service levels, limits, and fees belong in the order form.

## 7. Responsibilities

### Customer

- Supply authorized users, approved data, environment access, and timely decisions.
- Maintain its own identity, endpoint, network, and incident-response controls.
- Review recommendations independently before taking action.
- Preserve evidence according to the agreed custody and retention process.
- Report security events, scope changes, and suspected data issues promptly.
- Participate in scorecards and provide accurate, attributable feedback.

### Sentinel DNA

- Provide the agreed product scope, onboarding, documentation, and support contacts.
- Maintain tenant separation, auditability, evidence provenance, and fail-closed behavior within the agreed service boundary.
- Identify limitations and blocked workflows rather than masking them.
- Protect customer information according to the agreement and approved data handling.
- Provide a factual closeout report with methodology, evidence references, limitations, and open actions.

## 8. Success and exit criteria

Success is evaluated against the order form, not a generic benchmark. A pilot may be successful, partially successful, inconclusive, or blocked.

Minimum closeout evidence includes:

- The agreed workflows were attempted and the denominator is recorded.
- Analyst and customer feedback is attributable to a defined period and cohort.
- Evidence records have provenance, custody, reviewer, and disposition fields.
- Security and operational blockers are listed with owners and status.
- The customer sponsor makes a documented stop, extend, paid continuation, or subscription decision.

Exit occurs at the agreed date, when scope is completed, when a stop condition is met, or when either party terminates under the agreement. Exit requires access revocation and an evidence disposition record.

## 9. Pricing framework

Pricing is approved per package, scope, duration, users, environments, integrations, support, and data-handling requirements. The commercial owner must use the current approved price card and order-form template.

The proposal should distinguish:

- Pilot access or subscription fee.
- One-time onboarding or implementation services, if approved.
- Integration or premium support fees, if approved.
- Taxes, travel, or third-party costs, if applicable.
- Renewal or conversion pricing and any credits, only when approved in writing.

Do not insert illustrative prices into external collateral or report a forecast as revenue. A blank or `[Approved amount]` field means pricing is not yet approved.

## 10. Security and legal considerations

Security approval must cover data classes, access paths, tenant boundaries, logging, evidence custody, incident handling, deletion, and revocation. Legal review must cover confidentiality, data processing, intellectual property, liability, warranties, use restrictions, publication, fees, termination, and governing terms.

The pilot agreement must preserve human decision authority, prohibit credential storage and unauthorized authentication automation, and state the limitations of AI-generated recommendations. Legal and security approval are gates, not administrative afterthoughts.

## 11. Decision gates

| Gate | Required decision | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Qualification | Design Partner is eligible | Selection record and sponsor confirmation |
| Readiness | Customer can start safely | Onboarding readiness record |
| Authorization | Commercial and legal scope is approved | Signed agreement/order form |
| Execution | Pilot remains within controls | Weekly operating record and audit evidence |
| Closeout | Customer chooses next state | Final scorecard and decision record |
| Expansion | Additional capability is justified | Approved business case and security review |
