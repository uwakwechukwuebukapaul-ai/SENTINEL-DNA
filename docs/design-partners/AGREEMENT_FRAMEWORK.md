# Design Partner Agreement Framework

**Status:** Structure for legal, privacy, security, and commercial review
**Not an executed agreement**

## Relationship to other documents

The design partner agreement should identify the order of precedence among:

1. master services or commercial agreement, if any;
2. design partner statement of work;
3. NDA or confidentiality agreement;
4. data processing/privacy terms, if applicable;
5. security addendum;
6. pilot transition or paid-pilot proposal.

Legal counsel must resolve conflicts before access or paid work begins.

## Required structure

### 1. Parties, purpose, and term

Identify the legal parties, authorized representatives, purpose, start/end
dates, renewal/extension process, and governing documents.

### 2. Scope and deliverables

Define the approved use case, synthetic/sanitized dataset boundary, tenant,
analyst roles, scenarios, meetings, feedback outputs, evidence review, and
excluded activities. State that scope changes require written approval.

### 3. NDA relationship

Reference the applicable NDA, define precedence, cover non-public product and
program information, and identify permitted recipients. Do not assume an NDA
authorizes customer data or production access.

### 4. Data usage rules

- Synthetic data is the default.
- Sanitized data requires written approval, documented sanitization,
  re-identification risk review, retention, and deletion terms.
- Production, customer, personal, regulated, credential, cookie, token, and
  browser-session material is excluded unless a separately approved agreement
  explicitly changes the scope.
- Neither party may use pilot data for unrelated training, marketing, resale,
  or public disclosure without written approval.

### 5. Feedback and ownership

Define whether feedback is assigned, licensed, or retained by the partner;
whether Sentinel DNA may use de-identified aggregate insights; treatment of
pre-existing materials; and approval for quotes, logos, references, case
studies, or public statements.

### 6. IP protection

Protect each party's pre-existing IP, define deliverables and improvements,
address third-party materials, and avoid granting rights broader than the
engagement requires. Any product change remains subject to Sentinel DNA
engineering and security review.

### 7. Security requirements

Require least privilege, named users, private access, synthetic-data controls,
tenant isolation, audit logging, evidence custody, incident notification,
access revocation, and compliance with approved acceptable-use rules. Prohibit
credential sharing, control bypass, unauthorized testing, and public exposure.

### 8. Confidentiality boundaries

Define confidential information, exclusions, compelled disclosure, recipient
controls, incident handling, return/deletion, retention, and survival. Align
with the NDA rather than assuming identical terms.

### 9. Human decision authority

State that partner analysts remain responsible for their final conclusions and
that Sentinel DNA system or AI output is advisory. The agreement must not
authorize autonomous security, access, remediation, or customer decisions.

### 10. Fees and expenses

Design-partner participation may be unpaid, compensated, or otherwise
structured only as approved. The agreement must state payment, expenses, taxes,
invoicing, and no-purchase-obligation language where applicable.

### 11. Pilot transition clauses

Moving from design partner to paid pilot requires:

- validation evidence and unresolved-risk review;
- a separate proposal and acceptance decision;
- new scope, pricing, support, data, and service terms;
- customer security/procurement approval;
- no automatic conversion based on participation or feedback.

### 12. Termination and exit

Include suspension/termination rights, security stop events, withdrawal,
revocation, evidence handoff, deletion, payment treatment, and post-termination
confidentiality.

### 13. Liability and legal review

Counsel must address warranties, liability, indemnity, insurance, export and
sanctions rules, privacy, data residency, governing law, dispute process, and
electronic execution.
