## Purpose

The Sentinel DNA Design Partner Program converts selected FAVP participants
into structured partners who help evaluate Sentinel DNA workflows,
operational fit, evidence quality, and commercial requirements. The program is
designed to produce disciplined learning and decision records before any paid
pilot proposal.

The program does not assert customer results, revenue, market adoption, or
security outcomes in advance. Every finding must be supported by the defined
scope, collected evidence, participant feedback, and stated limitations.

## Strategic intent

Design partners provide a bridge between analyst validation and an accountable
commercial evaluation. Their role is to:

- test real analyst workflows using synthetic or separately approved sanitized
  data;
- challenge assumptions about evidence, provenance, usability, access, and
  auditability;
- identify the conditions under which Sentinel DNA could be useful to a
  security organization;
- help define an appropriately bounded paid pilot;
- provide feedback without receiving control over product claims or release
  decisions.

Participation is not a reference, endorsement, assurance statement, or promise
of a commercial relationship.

## Non-negotiable boundaries

- Analysts remain final decision makers; Sentinel DNA and AI output is advisory.
- The initial design-partner environment uses synthetic data only unless a
  separately approved scope authorizes sanitized data.
- Access is named, least-privileged, time-limited, tenant-scoped, and revocable.
- Evidence and provenance are captured without credentials, cookies, tokens,
  private keys, browser sessions, or unapproved customer data.
- Tenant isolation, audit logging, and evidence custody are required controls.
- A security, data, scope, or provenance concern pauses the engagement.
- No production runtime, customer access, public endpoint, or automatic paid
  conversion is created by this program.

## Program objectives

1. Identify the highest-value analyst workflows to evaluate.
2. Determine whether evidence and provenance are sufficient for reviewable
   decisions in the defined scenarios.
3. Expose control, workflow, usability, and integration gaps.
4. Measure trust, usage, evidence quality, time, and commercial intent using
   published definitions and honest denominators.
5. Establish evidence-based criteria for a paid pilot proposal.

## Engagement lifecycle

1. **FAVP review:** identify eligible participants from documented records.
2. **Discovery:** confirm problem, analyst workflow, organization, and
   decision owner.
3. **Qualification:** score analyst fit, organizational readiness, conflicts,
   and engagement capacity.
4. **Agreement:** complete NDA, design-partner terms, data, IP, feedback, and
   disclosure review.
5. **Onboarding:** establish bounded access, synthetic tenant, scenarios,
   evidence, audit, and revocation controls.
6. **Validation:** run the approved scenario and feedback plan.
7. **Synthesis:** review evidence, findings, objections, limitations, and
   commercial requirements.
8. **Decision:** continue, narrow, pause, exit, or prepare a paid-pilot
   proposal.

## Success criteria

Success is phase-specific and must be approved before measurement. Candidate
criteria include:

- agreed scenarios completed or formally closed;
- evidence and provenance sufficient for the stated review purpose;
- tenant, access, audit, and revocation controls observed and recorded;
- partner feedback is actionable and reproducible;
- analyst conclusions and disagreements remain visible;
- commercial needs and decision criteria are understood by the buyer or sponsor;
- open risks have owners and re-test conditions.

These criteria do not imply that the program has met them.

## Exit criteria

The program owner may pause or end an engagement when scope, security, data,
availability, conduct, evidence, legal, or commercial conditions cannot be
maintained. Other exits include completion of the validation question,
participant withdrawal, expired agreement, unresolved incident, or a decision
that no paid pilot is appropriate.

Every exit requires access revocation, post-revocation verification where in
scope, evidence handoff, findings disposition, and a written decision.

## Reporting and claims control

Internal reports must distinguish observed facts, analyst feedback, reviewer
interpretation, and open hypotheses. External material requires owner approval,
de-identification where applicable, and evidence review. No partner name, logo,
quote, result, conversion, revenue, or performance claim may be used without
the applicable written approval and supporting record.
