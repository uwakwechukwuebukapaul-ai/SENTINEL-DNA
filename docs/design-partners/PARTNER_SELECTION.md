# Design Partner Selection and Qualification

## Purpose

This document converts eligible FAVP participants into a small, accountable
design partner cohort. Selection is based on documented fit and willingness to
provide evidence-backed feedback, not on prestige, logo value, or assumed
commercial outcome.

## Selection principles

- start with FAVP participants whose participation records are complete and
  whose feedback is independent and constructive;
- select for workflow relevance, security maturity, and capacity to engage;
- avoid conflicts that could compromise feedback or create procurement
  pressure;
- require synthetic-data and confidentiality commitments before access;
- document every selection decision and its evidence;
- do not treat participation as an endorsement or market reference.

## Analyst qualification framework

Score each dimension from 0 to 3:

| Score | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | No evidence or material concern. |
| 1 | Limited evidence; substantial support would be required. |
| 2 | Relevant, evidenced capability with manageable gaps. |
| 3 | Strong, directly evidenced fit for the proposed design-partner work. |

| Dimension | Evidence to review |
| --- | --- |
| Security-analysis practice | FAVP participation, role context, scenario relevance. |
| Evidence discipline | Ability to cite sources, preserve provenance, and state limitations. |
| Independent judgment | Clear conclusions before or apart from advisory output. |
| Boundary discipline | Respect for tenant, access, synthetic-data, and stop conditions. |
| Feedback quality | Specific, reproducible, prioritized observations. |
| Communication | Ability to explain findings to technical and security stakeholders. |
| Availability | Confirmed time commitment for the proposed phase. |
| Conflict independence | Disclosed and resolved conflicts. |

an automatic admission rule. Any override requires written rationale and
security-owner review.

## Organizational readiness assessment

Assess the organization separately from the individual analyst:

- named executive or security sponsor;
- clearly bounded evaluation use case;
- ability to nominate one accountable analyst and backup contact;
- permission to use synthetic or approved sanitized data only;
- security, privacy, procurement, and legal review path;
- willingness to provide structured feedback and review evidence;
- ability to attend onboarding, validation, and closeout sessions;
- incident, access-revocation, and escalation contact;
- realistic commercial evaluation owner, without a pre-committed purchase;
- no request for unsupported claims, public references, or customer data use.

Status each item `PASS`, `BLOCKED`, or `NOT_MEASURED`. Organizational readiness
does not imply product readiness.

## Partner tiers

| Tier | Definition | Access | Commercial posture |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FAVP participant | Completed or active FAVP participant with feedback record. | FAVP scope only. | No commercial assumption. |
| Design partner candidate | Meets qualification and organizational-readiness review. | Proposed design-partner scope after approval. | Discovery and validation discussion. |
| Active design partner | Agreement, security review, scope, and onboarding complete. | Bounded design-partner environment and scenarios. | Structured feedback exchange; no purchase requirement. |
| Paid-pilot candidate | Validation evidence and business case support a paid-pilot proposal. | Proposed paid-pilot scope, subject to approval. | Commercial proposal; no promise of acceptance. |
| Paid pilot customer | Separate paid-pilot agreement and commercial approval executed. | Contracted pilot scope only. | Paid pilot under approved terms. |

Tier labels describe program state, not quality or external assurance.

## Engagement lifecycle

1. Identify eligible FAVP participants.
2. Obtain consent to contact and review the FAVP record.
3. Conduct qualification and organizational-readiness review.
4. Present scope, terms, confidentiality, and data boundaries.
5. Execute the design partner agreement and complete onboarding.
6. Run the approved validation plan and weekly feedback cadence.
7. Review evidence, findings, commercial fit, and unresolved risks.
8. Decide whether to continue, narrow, propose a paid pilot, or exit.

## Success criteria

Success must be defined per partner and phase before work starts. Candidate
criteria include:

- agreed scenarios completed with evidence references;
- tenant, access, audit, and provenance controls observed as intended;
- feedback specific enough to reproduce or triage;
- material limitations and disagreements recorded;
- partner and Sentinel DNA owners agree on the next decision;
- commercial interest, if any, documented by the appropriate buyer and not
  inferred from analyst enthusiasm.

Use `PASS`, `PARTIAL`, `BLOCKED`, or `NOT_MEASURED`; do not backfill missing
results.

## Exit criteria

Exit or pause when:

- agreed validation questions have been answered or are no longer useful;
- scope, data, security, or availability cannot be maintained;
- a partner requests unsupported access or claims;
- repeated feedback is not actionable after clarification;
- the partner withdraws or the agreement expires;
- a security incident or unresolved boundary failure requires termination;
- the commercial case is not supported by evidence or stakeholder alignment.

Exit includes access revocation, evidence handoff, findings disposition, and a
written decision. No exit status implies a purchase, reference, or endorsement.
